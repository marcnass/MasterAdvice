## Study Plan for Robotics <a name="StudyPlan"></a>
The Robotics Master's has 120 credits, including 2 (or 3) 10-credit Semester Projects, an uncredited internship and a 30-credit Master's Project (which can be done in industry also).

My plan upto present
- MA1: 33 credits
- MA2: 28 credits, incl. Semester Project
- MA3: 24 credits, incl. Semester Project (lighter than needed)
- MA4: Internship + 7 credits (2 overboard, had to get special approval from the section)

## My Projects <a name="Projects"></a>

### Semester Project 1

I did my first Semester Project at DDMAC, with Prof. Karimi, my TA was Vaibhav Gupta. It was a project in control, very much retracing the things taught in Advanced Control Systems. 

- The TA was helpful, but busy, a high degree of independence was expected
- Prof. was absent, not overly strict in grading.
- Work was simulation*
- Mainly worked in the lab, but could've been almost fully remote

My project was a bit of a special case, as it was supposed to be a more hardware-oriented project, but I had to re-orient it to more theory/simulation since the real-time system wasn't working. Overall, it was a bit more work than it was worth, and the results weren't super satisfying, but the lab atmosphere in the Control Lab is really cool, and the TAs were happy to help whenever they could! In what I've seen, DDMAC is where you go for more theoretical Control Projects, Ferrari-Trecate or Jones might be better if you want to apply things (you can never guarantee success in a project, keep this in mind)!

### Semester Project 2

My second Semester Project was done with EPFL Xplore and supervised by Prof. Alexandre Schmid. It involved Firmware Programming for a custom PCB made, in parallel, by a friend.

- Doing a project as part of a MAKE association means you're entirely on your own, and Prof. Schmid specifically did not get involved in the project at all, beside the presentation and final report, only do this if you want full autonomy.
- I had to define for myself the project, its scope and its expected results.
- With the end of the semester, it was tough deciding what work to present and discuss, and what work didn't really belong in a report.

I'd recommend having a go at working with a MAKE project, at least for a moment during your EPFL stay, it's super fun, you'll meet a lot of different people and learn skills you can't really find in courses! Landing a Semester Project at a MAKE project is tougher, you'll need to find a TL/SE who's willing to follow your work, and find a subject both useful enough to contribute to the project's advancement, and that can provide a basis for writing a report and presenting your work (you can't make a project out of cable management xD). My project coming about was the result of fortunate circumstances first and foremost, but Xplore, the Rocket Team, and many other MAKE projects constantly offer up semester projects on their websites, or you can go talk to members and team leaders to learn more about it (you'll always find someone wearing their merch!)

## My Internships <a name="Internships"></a>
### Finding an Internship
I started searching for an internship in mid-MA2, but couldn't find what I wanted. It was however very helpful to have tried before my MA3, as I got interview experience and was able to get some feedback about what to do, and how to fix my CV and write a motivation letter -- asking the interviewer how YOU did is probably the most important thing to ask IMO.

When MA3 rolled around, I attended Forum EPFL, and learned about many companies doing things I liked. Many of them already had all their internship proposals up on IS-Academia, others still had you apply through their website, and overall I didn't find my internship there, but it helped me decide what I was looking for. Melexis had a stand there, but were proposing internships I didn't specifically care for, but by asking the engineers there, they told me they had other things on their online portal, so I promptly applied for everything on there. 

### The Interviews
During my interview, I got a couple technical questions, but also questions about my previous projects and courses, about my interests and what I wanted to learn. I ended up landing an internship for a subject I'm definitely not the best at, but it was the one I really wanted to learn more about!

In total, I applied to about 20 different internships, only getting about 10 answers, I interviewed 7 different times, some interviews were very short, with me or the recruiter quickly realising it wasn't the position for me, and only one interview ever had me go through a second round (wasn't even taken). If I were in CompSci or Finance, I definitely would've had a much harder time, but I never had to do leetcode problems or the such, most interviews are just a discussion between you and the recruiter about what you've done, what the project is, and what you know and don't know about it. Bullshit won't take you far, and "I don't know, but I want to learn more" is what got me my internship, but definitely don't undersell yourself either: just having heard of something may be all they want from you in some cases!

### Paperwork
I'm not Swiss or European, and I landed an internship in France, so I had some paperwork to get through. All in all, it really wasn't that bad. You should probably consult [the relevant website](https://sti.epfl.ch/smt/internships/) for actual information.

- Everybody needs to sign the **EPFL internship agreement**, and make sure it's filled properly!
- You also still need to register on IS-Academia, but you need to update your status! If you don't plan on taking any courses during the internship, you pay much less for the semester. If you plan on taking courses during the internship, you'll need to get them validated, and pay the full enrollment!
- I had to apply for the Visa myself, but I found the Consulate in Geneva much easier to deal with, much more flexible with issues and errors, and much faster at responding and delivering the visa, than ever in my experience at home.


### Internship Experience
My internship had me working on Hardware Safety, a niche topic in overall ASIC design, but still meant doing digital design and verification, which meant I got to develop the rudimentary knowledge I had in those fields. I got to learn a bunch about working in digital design, and met a lot of cool people too! 

Of course, it wasn't 100% sunshine and roses, but it was a lot of fun, very educational, and I ended up signing on for a Master Thesis in Industry with them for next semester!


## Other Activities <a name="Other"></a>
Was at EPFL Xplore during my MA2-3, which was fun! But starting W9, I didn't really have the time to work as much as I should have. Beware the MA2 end of semester crunch, I guess!

I did many Student Assistant jobs for Bachelor's courses:

- Microfabrication Technologies: Wrote the Jupyter Notebook exercises. Was fun work, but I don't think more than 5 people bothered to do them!
- Ingenierie Optique: Prof. Martin was looking for extra assistants, but it was super light work, as very few students actually bothered coming to the sessions. 
- Automatique et Commande Numerique: This is actually how I got in contact with Vaibhav for the Semester Project, and it's not easy explaining control!
- Microcontroleurs (SEL): Pretty much the worst money/effort ratio SA job at EPFL, as you're expected to grade the weekly labs, supervise and grade the exam, and grade the project. It's not an easy course either, and many students don't really know where to start with the project. Wasn't my first time doing this, and Prof. Schmid keeps recruiting more and more assistants each year, making the job easier and more worthwhile.
- Systems Programming for SoC: My first time assisting a class of less than a hundred people, exercise sessions were chill and fun. I was expected to also grade Practical Works and supervise the exam, which wasn't bad!
- Image Processing I: A more traditional assistant assignment, the course isn't really tough so I got a fun time out of answering relevant questions and explaining the concepts!
- Basics of Mobile Robotics: Well paid, with many opportunities for extra work on the side! Prof. Mondada is happy to recruit many assistants, and he's got a good system for organizing exercise sessions, so you're never overwhelmed. By the end of the semester it does turn into a 3-hour project question fest, but it's fun!
- Systemes Embarques et Robotique: Well paid! But 5 consecutive, intense hours of work aren't easy! It was a lot of fun, especially durings the projects, but so much work!
